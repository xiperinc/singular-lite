01 / Start here

Two budgets meet at
one line of Bash.

Review rounds bound how often a change may be judged. Repair passes bound how often it may be rewritten. They are different mechanisms with different keys — and one clamp couples them.

This report audits the shipped implementation against the live tree: engine/review_policy.py (1,191 lines), the driver engine/l1-drive.sh, engine/task_lifecycle.py, the frozen campaign policy config-A16.json, and the durable ledger at .singular-state/review-policy/ledger.json — nine logical changes, nineteen recorded rounds, four exceptions, and a 33-item non-blocking backlog. Every behavioural claim in sections 03 and 04 was reproduced by calling the live module, not inferred from reading it.

One attempt, six stations, three budget domains. Transport failures never reach the review ledger.

The clamp collapses the risk tier. After the tier resolves, the driver caps repairs at maxReviewRounds − 1. With the shipped ceiling of two rounds that is one repair — so a Risk tier: high task receives exactly what a normal task receives, and its risk-derived ceiling of two is unreachable. All eighteen l1.review_policy_bound events in this campaign carry the same pair: maxReviewRounds 2, productRepairMax 1.

02 / Programmability

Everything is configurable.
Not everything is equally privileged.

Both budgets resolve through a strict precedence chain. They do not resolve through the same one, and the difference is the point.

  ↓
campaign config

      SINGULAR_REVIEW_POLICY_JSON

SINGULAR_MAX_RETRIES may only LOWER the
risk-derived ceiling, never raise it.

The privilege asymmetry. The task contract is a first-class input to the repair budget and has no input at all to the review budget. An author changes the repair tier by editing one markdown line; raising review rounds requires a CLI grant carrying --authority and at least one hashed evidence file. The direction is right — and worth stating, because the two headers sit three lines apart in the same contract.

What is hardcoded

Four things are configurable from no layer, by design: the severity vocabulary P0–P3; the verdict vocabulary accepted / needs-fix / blocked / needs-human; the evidence rule compiled into classify() — a P0 or P1 must carry trigger, impact and requirement; and the clamp, a literal in the driver. A grant may never exceed maxReviewRounds in one exception. An invalid value at any layer is a hard exit 2 — the driver refuses to spend money on a run whose policy will not load, rather than falling back to a default.

03 / Ledger and state integrity

The ledger keys on the change,
not on the task.

That single decision is why review history survived three task supersessions in this campaign — and it is also where the one real reset vector lives.

memory-lifecycle          status=accepted   rounds=3   exceptions=2
  1  TASK-1105  needs-fix   (historical, backfilled)
  2  TASK-1105  needs-fix   (historical, backfilled)
  3  TASK-1117  accepted
  exc-cc6aba1a1be9  +2  bound TASK-1116   authorized, never used
  exc-68487d55c6f8  +2  bound TASK-1117   the migration grant

Live excerpt. Three task identities, one continuous budget carried by the node.

Writes are fcntl-locked and atomic; read verbs take a shared lock, create no state and never rewrite, so updatedAt keeps meaning “last recorded change”. Backfilled rounds carry historical: true and still count toward used — provenance without free budget. Exceptions are task-bound by default, so the two grants above could never combine: the migration ceremony the supervisor performed was enforced by the code, not merely observed by it.

Can a task reset its budget?

VectorResets?Why
Rename the branchNoThe branch name appears in no key. Renaming or recreating a worker branch has zero ledger effect.
New task idNoWith a DAG node declared, the successor inherits the node’s full history. Verified live: invocation-envelope holds both TASK-1115 rounds.
Drop the DAG nodeYeslogical_change_id() falls back to taskId when dagNode is blank. A fresh budget is minted under a new key, with no grant and no authority.
unparkPartlyResets retryCount and clears productPassStarted; does not touch the review ledger. Re-entry buys worker passes, never review rounds.

The reset vector lives in the least-privileged file in the system. A task contract is ordinary markdown that any author edits. Deleting or retyping its DAG node: line moves the most durable counter in the engine. Everything else that can extend a budget demands an authority string and hashed evidence.

Acceptance closes the candidate — and zeroes the count

_open_round_count() resets used to zero at every accepted verdict, so a later dispatch of the same node starts fresh at two. The intended “acceptance closes the candidate” rule, visible in the live ledger:

maintenance:a12-engine-boundary   needs-fix → accepted → needs-fix
                                  used 1 · allowed 2 · status open

The consequence: the ceiling is per candidate, not per node. A node that alternates accept and needs-fix has no cumulative bound. Defensible — each acceptance is a real gate passage — but not what “max 2 rounds” reads like in a config file.

04 / Correctness

Where it holds,
and the three traps.

The accounting is honest and the gates fire in the right order. The failures that remain are all in one place: what happens when a verdict is malformed rather than wrong.

5

rounds recorded in the Claude phase — one per real verdict. No timeout, session-limit kill or provider failure produced a round.

33

P2/P3 findings diverted to backlog.ndjson instead of blocking a merge, across nine logical changes.

0

severity downgrades fired in the whole campaign. Trap 1 below is latent, not historical.

Working exactly as intended

The pre-audit check is the best design decision in the module: exhaustion is discovered before a paid call. Fail-closed classification works — a needs-fix carrying only prose findings synthesises unclassified-N items that are blocking, so an auditor cannot escape a fix by skipping the taxonomy. Every round stamps the reviewer’s runner, model and effort, which made this campaign’s provenance claims checkable afterwards.

TRAP 01

A genuine P0 can be downgraded into an acceptance

classify() requires trigger, impact and requirement to be non-blank on any P0 or P1, and demotes to P2 otherwise. The v1 schema requires only id, severity and summary — the three supporting fields are optional. So a schema-valid verdict exists in which the auditor reports a real P0 and the host converts the run to accepted. Reproduced against the live module:

input : verdict = needs-fix
        F1  severity=P0  summary + trigger + impact   (no requirement)
output: effectiveVerdict = accepted
        downgraded = [F1]        applied = true

Nothing blocks, the round records as accepted, integration proceeds. The rule is defensible as an anti-inflation measure — but “the claim is unsupported” and “the verdict is now accepted” should not be the same branch.

TRAP 02

Legacy verdicts validate in warn mode

SINGULAR_AUDIT_VERDICT_VALIDATE defaults to warn. A v1 verdict is always fail-closed, but a v0 or legacy-schema verdict that fails validation emits l1.audit_verdict_warned and proceeds to be recorded and to influence acceptance. Campaign A16 runs the v1 contract, so it was never exposed. A consumer repository still on v0 is.

TRAP 03

Schema failures never reach the review budget at all

A packet or verdict that cannot be parsed is an infrastructure class, so it consumes worker-repair budget while the review ledger stays untouched. That is the TASK-1114 failure in one sentence: a complete candidate — later audited clean and integrated — spent its entire product budget on two missing createdAt fields and parked, having never been reviewed once. The 0.22.0 repair pass and the 0.23.0 createdAt default close the two known shapes; the structural gap, a format slip billed to the product budget, is still open.

05 / Recommendations

Three minimal changes,
each independent of the others.

None requires new durable state, a new verb, or a migration. Two are single-function edits; the third is a classification move.

01

Never let a downgrade manufacture an acceptance

In classify(), when downgraded is non-empty and no blocking item remains, set effectiveVerdict to needs-human — already a valid verdict in the vocabulary — instead of accepted. Roughly four lines, no schema change, no new state.

Alternatively, or in addition: make those three fields required in the v1 schema for P0 and P1 items. That moves the failure into the auditor’s own validation-repair loop, where a malformed verdict already belongs.

02

Make the logical-change key non-forgeable

Require a DAG node: header in any contract that declares a Target branch:, and refuse dispatch when it is absent rather than falling back to taskId. Where a successor legitimately needs a new node, grant --authority is already the mechanism for that. This closes the only budget-reset vector a task author can reach unaided.

03

Give a format slip its own budget

Classify packet-invalid and worker-no-packet on an unchanged candidate as an infrastructure domain with its own single retry, rather than charging the product-repair budget. The park rule added in 0.22.0 already grants one re-emit; this makes the accounting match the intent. It is the change that would have saved TASK-1114 outright.

Two smaller follow-ons. Flip SINGULAR_AUDIT_VERDICT_VALIDATE to strict by default, keeping warn as an explicit opt-out for v0 consumers. And decide the coupling in section 01 deliberately: if high-risk tasks are meant to get two repairs, maxReviewRounds must be 3, because today the clamp erases the distinction the risk tier exists to make.

What this audit does and does not establish

ESTABLISHED

The precedence chains, the ledger keying and the three traps are properties of the shipped code, reproduced by direct invocation on 2026-09-16.

NOT ESTABLISHED

That trap 01 has ever fired — zero downgrades are recorded. That the policy holds under concurrent dispatch: this campaign ran one driver at a time.
